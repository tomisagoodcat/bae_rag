## A B S T R A C T

Irrespective of cost and ecological risk, literatures have reported that both biochar and selenium (Se) alone at high application rate exhibited positive effects on decreasing rice mercury (Hg) uptake in high Hg contaminated paddy soil. In this study, we investigated whether biochar and Se together at low dose could efficiently reduce the rice grain Hg and MeHg accumulation in the slight Hg-contaminated soil. Compared with control (CK), the Hg concentration of grains in the BC3, Se0.5, and BC3 + Se0.5 treatments decreased by 5.4 %, 38.3 %, and 48.5 %, respectively. Co-application of biochar and Se also decreased the methylmercury (MeHg) concentration in rice grains by 29.1 -91.6 %. The decrease of Hg and MeHg level in rice grains for biochar and Se treatments could be attributed to the following mechanisms: (1) high Hg (primarily inorganic Hg) adsorption on biochar through its high hydroxyl groups and large specific surface area; (2) Increased dissolved organic carbon and cysteine contents in pore water after biochar application, which reduced the availability of soil Hg through complexation; (3) Decreased bioavailability of Hg in soil due to the formation of HgSe precipitation which inhibited Hg uptake and translation by rice plant; (4) Both biochar and Se facilitated the reduction of MeHg in soil. Our results indicate that co-application of biochar and Se at low dose is a promising method to effectively mitigate Hg accumulation in rice grains from the slight Hg-contaminated soil.

## 1. Introduction

Mercury (Hg) has been a global concern due to its methylation to more toxic methylmercury (MeHg) driven by microorganisms, which can be bioaccumulated and biomagnificated in food chain (Seelen et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023; Man et al., 2024). Rice was an important source of MeHg exposure (Zhang et al., 2010), widely existing in many countries, such as Indonesia, Pakistan, United States, India, and China. In China, 7 -96 % of MeHg exposure was originated from rice consumption (Gong et al., 2018). Therefore, reducing the production and bioavailability of MeHg in paddy soil is crucial to alleviate the accumulation of MeHg in rice grain.

At present, several techniques have been applied for Hg remediation in agricultural soil, such as phytoremediation (Liu et al., 2020), thermal treatment (Qiu et al., 2014), water management (Ishfaq et al., 2021), and applying soil stabilization materials such as activated carbon (Bussan et al., 2016), sulphate (Liu et al., 2023), and Se (Chang et al., 2020). Phytoremediation needs a few years to remove soil Hg. Heating up to 350 ◦ C can improve the removal rate of Hg in soil by &gt; 90 % (Qiu et al., 2014), but the cost and effect on soil properties remain to be carefully considered. Peng et al. (2012) found that aerobic rice cultivation could reduce the Hg uptake by rice. However, the growth of rice plant can be affected under drought conditions because flooded environments ensure the fixation levels of higher nitrogen and phosphorus while alleviating the competition from soil-borne pathogens and weeds (Li et al., 2015). Therefore, developing an economical, green, and effective method to reduce the accumulation of MeHg in rice grain while ensuring rice production is highly demanded.

Biochar is an environmentally friendly multi-functional carbon material, which has been widely applied to lead, copper, cadmium, arsenic, and Hg immobilization in croplands (Boie et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024). The immobilization mechanisms include direct and indirect interactions, such as complexation, electrostatic attraction, surface chemisorption, and precipitation (e.g., metal (hydr)oxide, phosphate, and carbonate precipitates) (Deng et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2021). Adsorption is the primary mechanism for reducing the bioavailability of Hg in paddy soil. However, biochar could also cause the increment of MeHg in soil (Shu et al., 2016; Lv et al., 2021), increasing the risk of MeHg contamination in soil. In addition, selenium (Se) application was effective to control the mobilization and toxicity of Hg in soil and rice plant systems through forming the insoluble complexes with inorganic Hg and MeHg in rhizosphere or roots (Zhang et al., 2012). Wang et al. (2019) showed that biochar reduced the MeHg accumulation of rice grain in Se applied paddy soil. Lv et al. (2021) also reported the positive effect of Se amended biochar on Hg and MeHg uptake by rice plants. The above experiments were mainly conducted in high Hg contaminated paddy soil, where the soil, air and water were severely contaminated by smelting activities in Wanshan Hg mining area located in Guizhou Province, China, and most of those studies required biochar application at high doses to reduce the Hg uptake by rice. The problems were that applying high amount of biochar caused high cost and was difficult to be accepted by farmers, and Se also had a narrow concentration range between deficiency and toxicity (Dang et al., 2019). The study on the coapplication effect of biochar and Se at low dosage in soil Hg remediation was sparse, especially in the slight Hg contaminated soil.

Therefore, this study aims to investigate (i) whether biochar could reduce the methylation capacity of Hg in the slight Hg contaminated soils; (ii) whether there was a synergistic effect between BC and Se at low dose in reducing the Hg and MeHg accumulation in rice grains; and (iii) the mechanism of biochar and Se in controlling the Hg and MeHg accumulation in rice grains.

## 2. Materials and methods

## 2.1. Soil, biochar, and selenium sources

The paddy topsoil (0 -15 cm) was collected from Jiangning District, Nanjing City, Jiangsu Province, China (31 ◦ 51 ′ N, 118 ◦ 52 ′ E). The collected soils were passed through 2 mm sieve for pot experiments and 0.15 mm sieve for incubation experiments, respectively. The soil Hg content was 0.8 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , exceeding the Hg risk screening value (Hg 0.6 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , 6.5 &lt; pH ≤ 7.5) in paddy soil (GB15618-2018). Other soil properties were as follows: soil pH 6.94, soil organic carbon 20.4 g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , Se content 0.4 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , and cation exchange capacity 17.3 cmol kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 .

The rice husk biochar was purchased from Nanjing Qinfeng Straw Technology Co., LTD. The biochar contained 55.2 % carbon, 2.0 % hydrogen, 9.6 % oxygen, 0.1 % sulfur, and 0.8 % nitrogen. Na2SeO3 (Se (IV)) was obtained from Sahn Chemical Technology Co., LTD. (Shanghai).

## 2.2. Pot experiments

## 2.3. Soil incubation experiments

To further study the dynamics of soil MeHg content in the biochar, Se, and their co-application treatments, soil incubation experiments were carried out with ten treatments. Briefly, forty grams of soil was added into 100 mL deionized water containing different dosage of biochar (calculated as biochar in soil at 7.5, 15, and 40 t ha GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ) or Se (0.5, 1, and 2 mg L GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ) in 250-mL dark brown glass bottles. Additionally, three co-application treatments (BC7.5 + Se0.5, BC7.5 + Se1, and BC40 + Se2) were performed to investigate the synergistic effect of biochar and Se on MeHg in soil. The detailed information was shown in Table 2. Afterwards, a total of 30 glass bottles were thoroughly shaken and then placed in an incubator at 25 ◦ C. On day 3, 5, 10, and 20, aliquots (3.3 mL) of soil slurry in each bottle were sampled under natural conditions after manual shaking. Cold vapor atomic fluorescence spectrometry (CVAFS) was used to measure the MeHg concentration in soil after the collected soil samples was treated following the process of centrifugal separation, freeze-dried, ground and passed through 0.15 mm sieves, and KOH-methanol digestion (Lv et al., 2021).

## 2.5. Data processing and statistics

The measured data were described by mean ± standard deviation. One way analysis of variance with Tukey-HSD test was used to analyze the difference between treatments, and the significant difference was considered at p &lt; 0.05 level. The calculated mass concentration of Hg and MeHg was based on dry weight. Lowercase letters from all figures represent significant difference between treatments.

## 3. Results

## 3.1. Accumulation of Hg and MeHg in rice grains

The average concentrations of Hg and MeHg for CK in rice grains were 53.6 and 34.8 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , respectively, exceeding the safety level of 20 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 for cereal grains (CNSA, 2022). After the application of rice husk biochar, Se, and their mixture, the concentration of Hg in rice grains was significantly decreased (Fig. 1 ). Compared with CK, the reduction percentage of Hg concentration in the rice grain of BC3 and Se0.5 treatments was 5.4 % and 38.3 %, respectively. However, the rice grain Hg concentration in the BC3 + Se0.5 treatments was significantly reduced by 48.5 % as compared to CK. Similar results were also observed in other co-application treatments.

The concentration of MeHg in rice grains also showed downward trend after the application of rice husk biochar, Se, and their mixture (Fig. 2 ). In the biochar treatments, the MeHg concentration in BC40 treatments was decreased by 69.3 % as compared to CK; In the Se treatments, compared with CK, the concentration of MeHg in the rice grains of Se0.5, Se1, Se1.5, and Se2 treatments decreased by 25.1 %, 38.1 %, 43.1 %, and 50.0 %, respectively. In co-application treatments, compared with BC3 treatments, the grain MeHg concentrations in the BC3 + Se0.5, BC3 + Se1, BC3 + Se1.5, and BC3 + Se2 were significantly decreased by 42.2 %, 38.3 %, 56.1 %, and 50.7 %, respectively. However, the grain MeHg concentration in the BC40 + Se2 treatment was 65.7 % lower than that in the BC40 + Se1.5 treatment, while there was no significant difference among the BC40, BC40 + Se0.5, BC40 + Se1, and BC40 + Se1.5 treatments.

## 3.2. The potential mechanisms of MeHg by rice husk BC and Se

The concentration of Hg in the recovered BC particles was 50.2 times than that in the fresh BC particles, but the MeHg concentration accounted for only 0.07 % (Fig. 3). And the results of three-dimensional excitation emission matrix fluorescence spectra showed that the humic acid-like substance was the primary composition in the pore water DOC for the biochar treatments (Fig. 5). ## 3.3. Soil MeHg dynamic changes in the incubation experiment

During the waterlogged incubation (Fig. 7 ), the soil MeHg concentration in the CK increased gradually as the incubation time increased from day 0 to 20. After flooding for 20 d, the soil MeHg concentration in CK increased by 180.1 % as compared with that at day 0. Similar increasing trends were observed in biochar, Se, and their co-application treatments. However, increasing amount of biochar gradually reduced the soil MeHg concentration from 3 to 20 d. For example, at day 20, the peak concentrations of MeHg for BC7.5, BC15, and BC40 treatments were decreased by 29 %, 35.6 %, and 60.7 %, respectively, and the soil MeHg concentrations were decreased by 26.3 % in the Se0.5 treatment, 36.3 % in the Se1 treatment, and 46.1 % in the Se2 treatment, as compared with CK. Increasing dose of Se quickly decreased the production of soil MeHg. From 0 to 3 d, the soil MeHg concentrations in the Se0, Se0.5, Se1, and Se2 treatments increased by 89.6 %, 91.4 %, 61.8 %, and GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 6.1 %, respectively. During 3 -20 d, the soil MeHg concentration in the CK treatment still increased gradually, while the MeHg kept stable in the Se0.5 treatments, and continuously decreased in the Se1 and Se2 treatments. The MeHg concentration was significantly lower in the co-application of biochar and Se treatments than in CK treatment after 3 d of MeHg production in soil. After 20 d of soil incubation, the MeHg concentration in the co-application of biochar and Se (BC7.5 + Se 0.5), was decreased by 5.7 % and 9.2 % than those in the BC7.5 and Se0.5 alone, respectively.

## 4. Discussion

## 4.1. Synergistic effect of BC and Se reduced Hg and MeHg accumulation in rice grain

In the present study, our results showed the synergistic effect of coapplication of BC and Se on reducing Hg and MeHg accumulation in rice grain, even at low application rate (3 t ha GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 biochar + 0.5 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 Se). The US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) recommended the limit of MeHg intake for human was 0.1 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 body weight per day (USEPA, 1997). For one with average body weight of 60 kg and daily rice intake of 250 g (Lv et al., 2021), the daily intake of MeHg associated with rice grains was 0.15 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 for the CK, 0.15 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 for BC3, 0.11 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 for Se0.5, and 0.096 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 for BC3 + Se0.5 treatment, suggesting that co-application of BC and Se significantly lowered the MeHg exposure risk through rice grains.

Similar results also showed that the rice grain Hg and MeHg concentrations were significantly lower in the co-application of BC and Se treatments than in the BC treatments (Wang et al., 2019; Lv et al., 2021). However, the dose and application method of BC and Se in the present study was different from the previous studies. Our present study designed the co-application treatments including lower dose of biochar (3 t ha GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ) and Se (0.5 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ), and the Se solution was just evenly sprayed on the surface of the overlying water after the soils and biochars were fully mixed and flooded. Wang et al. (2019) added the Na2SO3 (3 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 and 6 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 Se) into soils and aged these soils for 3 years firstly, and then 10 t ha GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 biochar was mixed into the soils and showed that biochar could further reduce the rice grain MeHg concentration in the Se amended soils. Lv et al. (2021) dissolved sodium selenite in water and mixed with biochar powder to prepare the Se amended biochar (Se 1000 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ), and found that the rice grain MeHg concentration in the BC treatments was significantly higher than BC + Se treatments, especially in 5 %-BC + Se treatments. However, the high dose of biochar and Se in the above studies would also cause high cost and ecological risk in the practical Hg contaminated soils although Lv et al. (2021) considered that 1 -5 % application rate of biochar was relatively low, and &gt; 3 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 Se would cause high risk for human health.

It was a little pity that the 3 t ha GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 (0.13 %) application rate of biochar was a bit lower, leading to the insignificant difference in the grain Hg or MeHg concentration between BC3 and CK treatments, and BC3 + different levels of Se also had little effect on the Hg content in the rice compared to adding Se alone. According to the related results in the previous studies, biochar application could effectively reduce Hg uptake by rice plants, while the application rate in most studies was relatively higher ( Table S1 ). A previous study also reported that low and high dose of biochar application (0.3 % -5 %) could all control the accumulation of MeHg in rice plants through reducing the soil MeHg bioavailability, even in a relatively lower dose of biochar (0.3 %) (Wang et al., 2023). Therefore, we suggested that biochar (0.3 %) + Se (0.5 mg kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ) might be considered as a low cost and low risk strategy in reducing Hg and MeHg accumulation in rice grains from the moderate and mild Hg contaminated soil.

## 4.2.1. The contribution of BC for the immobilization of Hg and MeHg in soils

In biochar treatments, the immobilization mechanism of Hg and MeHg by biochar in our study could be explained by three aspects: Firstly, BC could reduce the availability of inorganic Hg in soil through adsorption, controlling the conversion from inorganic Hg to MeHg. This was supported by the higher Hg concentration detected in the recovered biochar as compared to the fresh biochar, and inorganic Hg was the primary fraction in the recovered biochar (Fig. 3). Adsorption of MeHg and inorganic Hg by biochar in soil or sediment was also reported in a previous study (Gomez-Eyles et al., 2013). Similarly, Man et al. (2021) reported the decrease of exchangeable soil Hg, and specifically sorbed Hgconcentration induced by biochar could affect the formation of MeHg in soil. Xing et al. (2020) attributed the decrease of Hg concentration in polished rice to the complexation between MeHg and thiols in biochar added soils, because high levels of organosulfur groups in biochar had strong affinity in binding with Hg 2 + or MeHg + through chemical bonds (Ravichandran, 2004; Xing et al., 2020). Huang et al. (2024) found the enhancement capacity in soil Hg immobilization after thiol modified biochar were applied in soil. Unfortunately, we did not identify the organic sulfur containing functional groups (R-SH, 2500 -2700 cm GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ) in the FTIR of the fresh biochar and recovered biochar (Fig. S1), indicating that the contribution of thiols from biochar in complexing with MeHg is negligible in this study. The adsorption of inorganic Hg by biochar was due to the increased hydroxyl groups, specific surface area, and pore volume (Fig. S1, Table S1). Previous adsorption experiment also showed that high specific surface area and numerous oxygen-containing functional groups could enhance the adsorption capacity of rice straw biogas residue biochar on Hg, high specific surface area increased the active adsorption sites on biochar (Liu et al., 2022), and the complexation of phenolic hydroxyl (COH) and Hg 2 + contributed to the formation of ( -O2)Hg 2 + (Xu et al., 2016). Gomez-Eyles et al. (2013) also attributed the immobilization of MeHg in biochar treatments to the larger relative surface area of biochar, providing numerous binding sites for MeHg. However, we found the concentration of MeHg in recovered biochar particles was just 0.12 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , and thus the adsorption capacity of MeHg on biochar might be limited.

Secondly, we found that the decreased accumulation of Hg and MeHg in the rice grain of BC treatments was likely related to the increased DOC and cysteine concentrations in pore water (Fig. 4). The increased DOC content in pore water induced by BC amendments could also control the Hg availability (Hu et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2021; Xing et al., 2022). However, recent studies concluded that cysteine could facilitate MeHg uptake by roots and translocation to shoots (Hao et al., 2022; Man et al., 2024). In our study, the increase of cysteine and DOC in biochar treatments could complex with MeHg + and Hg 2 + through forming -SHgCH3 and -SHg bond in soil (Huang et al., 2019). Similar result was also reported by Xing et al. (2020) who considered that MeHg + could be complexed with thiols in soils and form complexes between MeHg + and thiol firstly (Skyllberg, 2008), and then mobility of the complexes was limited by the rice husk biochar. Therefore, the difference between previous study and our results may be due to the fact that biochar immobilized the complexes of MeHg-thiol and MeHg-DOC, thereby reducing MeHg uptake by rice plants. Moreover, a previous study also showed that the DOM associated thiols could control the bioavailability of soil MeHg to phytoplankton instead of DOC (Seelen et al., 2023). Therefore, the relationship of MeHg level in rice plants with DOC and cysteine in pore water was merited for further investigation.

Thirdly, our incubation experiments showed that BC could reduce the concentration of MeHg in soil. At initial period (0 -5 d), biochar reduced the production of MeHg in soil, and the decrease percentage depended on the dose of biochar application. Interestingly, our study also found that the soil MeHg concentration in the biochar treatments decreased as the incubation time increased, which was possibly due to the demethylation of soil MeHg, especially in the BC40 treatments. This phenomenon provided a new possible explanation for the decrease of MeHg uptake by biochar. The possible reason was that biochar could serve as electron shuttles to facilitate the sulfate reduction in soil under the flooded condition (Huang et al., 2023), which resulted in the reduction in Hg -methylating efficiency, while MeHg demethylation continues. 4.2.2. The contribution of soil Se application for the immobilization of Hg and MeHg in soils

Numerous studies have reported the antagonism between Se and Hg in fish, humans, microorganisms, poultry, rats, and other mammals (Yang et al., 2008; Khan and Wang, 2009; Bjerregaard et al., 2011; Bjerregaard and Christensen, 2012). In the present study, this antagonism was also observed in rice plant that increasing Se level was negatively correlated with inorganic Hg and MeHg concentration in rice grains (Fig. 6). Furthermore, the Se concentrations in rice grains was also significantly and inversely related to the concentrations of Hg and MeHg in rice grains, which was consistent with the result of Zhang et al. (2012). Besides, they also found that the antagonistic effect depended on the Se concentration in rice grain. When the Se concentrations in rice grain was &lt; 100 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , more variation was observed in MeHg and IHg concentrations of rice grain so that the phenomenon of antagonism was not apparent. However, when the concentration of Se in rice grain was &gt; 100 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , the accumulation of MeHg and IHg in rice grain decreased and showed inverse correlation with rice Se concentration. In addition, covalent bonds could be formed between Se and Hg occurring in roots or rhizospheres so that the bioavailability of MeHg and IHg in the rhizosphere soil were limited. Surprisingly, the results of incubation experiment showed that Se addition could not only alleviate the production of MeHg in soil, but also reduced the MeHg content in soil. During the initial period of incubation (0 -3 d), the MeHg produced at the fastest rate with decreased peak concentrations as the Se level increased. explanation was that Se reduced the production of MeHg, while MeHg demethylation continues. Another explanation was that addition of Se contributed to the demethylation process of MeHg in soil. The MeHg demethylation induced by Se was reported in fish (Wang and Wang, 2017), and they attributed to the reaction between selenoamino acid and MeHg through the formation of dimethylmercury and bis(merthylmercuric)selenide as intermediates, leading to the precipitation of HgSe as demethylation product (Khan and Wang, 2010). Moreover, the hepatic demethylation was also reported in mammals and birds (Joiris et al., 2001; Eagles-Smith et al., 2009), and Se could participate in the detoxification process through the production of HgSe and sequestrate into hepatic cells (Lailson-Brito et al., 2012). Until now, the MeHg elimination in fish induced by Se remained controversial. Bjerregaard et al. (2011) and Amlund et al. (2015) found significant increase of MeHg elimination in muscle after different species (seleno-methionine, selenite, and seleno-cystine) of Se uptake by fish, while other studies reported that the changes of MeHg retention in marine fish was not related to Se species (Dang and Wang, 2011). Therefore, the interactions between MeHg and Se in fish muscle were complicated and the inconsistent result might be ascribed to the fish species, Se chemical forms, and Se dosage. The mechanisms on Se enhanced the demethylation of MeHg in paddy soils remains exclusive and needs more future investigations.

## 4.2.3. The synergistic mechanism of BC and se in reducing hg and MeHg accumulation in rice grain

The synergistic mechanism of BC and Se in our study included three aspects. Firstly, the sum of the individual contributions of BC and Se could further reduce Hg and MeHg accumulation by rice plants, which was consistent with the study of Lv et al. (2021) who also showed the dual roles of BC and Se in BC + Se treatments. Secondly, the increased cysteine in the pore water induced by BC had the reducing capacity, and BC also could serve as electron shuttles (Huang et al., 2023), which could further facilitate the selenite reduction in soil under the flooded condition, promoting the formation of HgSe in biochar added soil. Thirdly, the fresh rice husk BC contained 0.1 % S, and application of S could further inhibit the MeHg uptake by plants in Se added soils under the flooded environment (Wang et al., 2016), and the possible explanation was due to the decreased MeHg phytoavailability after S application: the reduced S 2 GLYPH&lt;0&gt; under flooded soil environment could contribute to the formation of iron sulfide in soils, which reduced the phytoavailability of MeHg through surface complexation (Skyllberg and Drott, 2010). The most important was that BC and Se reduced the production of MeHg in soil but MeHg demethylation continues, leading to the lower Hg and MeHg accumulation in rice grains. Therefore, application of BC and Se at low dose is a promising method to effectively mitigate Hg accumulation in rice grains from the slight Hg-contaminated soil.

## 5. Conclusions

Our experiments showed that there was a positive synergistic effect between biochar and Se application for soil Hg remediation, even at low dosages of biochar and Se treatments. The mechanism of Hg and MeHg reduction in rice grain by biochar was ascribed to its improved adsorption capacity to inorganic Hg and increased DOC and cysteine concentration in pore water, controlling the production of MeHg in soil and pore water, respectively. The higher Hg (especially inorganic Hg) concentration in the recovered biochar particles was the direct evidence for the adsorption of inorganic Hg by biochar in soil, which was resulted from its increased hydroxyl groups and large specific surface area as compared with the fresh biochar. And Se level was significantly and negatively correlated with inorganic Hg and MeHg in rice grains. The incubation experiments showed that biochar and Se inhibited the methylation process of inorganic Hg initially, and then reduced the MeHg content in soil. The contribution of biochar facilitating the formation of HgSe and the S in biochar inhibiting MeHg uptake in Se added and flooded soils could deeply understand the synergistic mechanism of BC and Se in reducing Hg and MeHg accumulation in rice grain.
