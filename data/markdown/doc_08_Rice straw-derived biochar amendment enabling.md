## A B S T R A C T

Keywords: Rice straw Mass spectrometry Carbon sequestration Methylmercury Ethylmercury

We report an unrecognized but significant mechanism of synergistic carbon sequestration and mercury (Hg) alkylation in Hg-contaminated paddy soil. This study investigates the influence of rice straw (RS) and biochar (BC) amendments on carbon fraction transformation and Hg alkylation. Results revealed that BC amendment facilitated Protein and Lipid convert into Tannin, possibly by Anaerolineaceae, thereby promoting soil carbon sequestration; whereas RS amendment increased the conversion of Amino sugars and Carbohydrates into Lignin when comparing the BC and RS additions. The RS amendment cannot effectively increase soil carbon stocks due to  the  considerable  CH4 emissions.  Furthermore,  RS  significantly  facilitated  the  conversion  of  Hg  ions  to methylmercury, as indicated by an 88.7% reduction in Hg ions and an 81.6% increase in methylmercury levels compared to the control (CK). The BC also promotes the methylation of Hg, but the intensity is not as strong as the RS. Compared with the CK, both the RS and BC showed an inhibitory effect on Hg ethylation, with RS having a stronger inhibitory capacity. These findings, which comprehensively consider soil carbon sequestration, Hg methylation and ethylation, benefits strong support for the BC amendment as the most advantageous environmental  strategy  in  comparison  to  the  CK  and  RS  treatments.  This  study  discovers  that  BC  amendment  can simultaneously enhance soil carbon sequestration and reduce Hg alkylation, and reveals the process mechanism.

## 1. Introduction

Paddy  soil  exhibit  considerable  potential  for  sequestering  soil organic carbon (SOC), a crucial factor in enhancing rice productivity and mitigating global warming [1,2]. Simultaneously but unfortunately, rice consumption has been recognized as a principal pathway for mercury (Hg)  and  methylmercury  (MeHg)  exposure  to  residents,  with  MeHgcontaminated rice posing a serious threat to human health on a global scale [3 -5]. Therefore, increasing soil carbon stock and mitigating Hg alkylation simultaneously in paddy soil are urgently required.

Recent studies have shown that BC application can increase SOC and dissolved organic carbon (DOC) concentrations,  indicating  its  significant  long-term  potential  for  SOC sequestration  by  reducing  substrate  degradability  through  enhanced carbon  aromaticity  [1,13]. Against the above background, both RS and BC exhibit significant impacts on Hg methylation and soil carbon sequestration. However, the specific mechanisms underlying carbon sequestration in Hgcontaminated paddy soil after the addition of RS and BC remain unclear,  hindering progress towards achieving carbon neutrality in rice cultivation. Notably, Ethyl mercury (EtHg), the second most biologically toxic form of organic Hg after MeHg, is a prevalent Hg species with high bioaccumulation potential, thus posing a significant threat to human health  [20,21].  Despite  the  predominant  focus  on  Hg  and  MeHg  in existing  studies,  the  level  and  transformation  of  EtHg  in  paddy  soil remain  poorly  understood,  leading  to  significant  uncertainties  in assessing the toxicity of organic Hg and predicting Hg behavior in soil. Therefore, there is an urgent need to investigate soil carbon sequestration and EtHg transformation in paddy soil with high background Hg level.

Additionally, dissolved organic matter (DOM) plays a crucial role in both soil carbon pool and Hg conversion [22,23]. The labile organic carbon fraction (e.g.,  carbohydrate  and  protein)  can  be  degraded  by Anaerolinea, significantly impacting on soil carbon sequestration [24]. Moreover, organic matter components characterized by lower aromatic content,  higher  oxygenation, and nitrogen richness in low molecular weights ( &lt; 0.5 kDa) may act as labile carbons for microbial methylators, potentially  enhancing  the  abundances  of  Geobacter  (anaerobic  Hg methylator) in paddy soils and promoting Hg methylation [25,26]. The above DOM-bacteria interactions highlighting the critical importance of utilizing mass spectrometry for precise analysis of labile organic carbon fractions, thereby advancing our comprehension of these geochemical processes.

Here, this study hypothesizes that DOM-bacteria interactions may facilitate the processes of soil carbon sequestration and Hg alkylation, and that the increase in stable carbon molecules can serve as an indicator of soil carbon sequestration. The research aims to enhance the understanding  of  carbon  sequestration  at  a  molecular  level  in  Hgcontaminated paddy soil and elucidate the variations in EtHg level, a pivotal aspect in Hg speciation transformation. To achieve this, a pot experiment  was  conducted  to  examine  the  impact  of  RS  and  BC amendments on molecular-level SOC sequestration. Subsequently, we measured  the  concentration  of  Hg  speciation,  and  characterized  Hg methylating communities by quantifying and sequencing hgcA ( DeltahgcA and Archaea-hgcA )  genes.  Additionally,  we  also  analyzed  the overall bacterial community by sequencing of 16S rRNA gene amplicons. The integrated findings provide novel insights into molecular-level SOC  sequestration,  as  well  as  Hg  speciation  transformation  in  Hgcontaminated paddy soils responding to RS and BC inputs, which are increasingly  concerned  due  to  intensive  agriculture  for  food  security demands.

## 2. Materials and methods

## 2.1. Paddy soil, RS and BC

Surface paddy soil (0 -20 cm) was collected from a Hg-contaminated rice field (29 ◦ 58 ́ N, 121 ◦ 31 ́ E) in Zhejiang Province, China. The soil was classified as clayey illitic thermic typic epiaquepts, a waterlogged paddy soil type based on the China Soil Classification System. After return to the laboratory, the paddy soil was air-dried, ground, sieved through a 2 mm mesh, and used in the pot and incubation experiments described

RS was collected from a site free of any known Hg contamination, and  was  washed,  air-dried,  and  ground.  In  China,  before  straw  is returned  to  the  fields,  it  is  commonly  chopped  up  to  enhance  its decomposition in situ [27]. Thus, in our study, the straw was ground prior to its use. Low-Hg straw was used to minimize potential interference by Hg-containing straw on soil MeHg levels. The concentrations of total Hg, MeHg and EtHg in the RS were 4.09 ± 0.31 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , &lt; 0.2 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , &lt; 0.2 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , respectively. BC was produced through the slow pyrolysis of rice straw biomass (particle size: &lt; 2 mm) in a cylindrical reactor, purged with nitrogen (N2), and maintained at 400 ◦ C for 2 h, with a heating rate of 10 ◦ C per minute. The resulting biochar was obtained in powdered form and filtered through a 10-mesh sieve prior to being thoroughly mixed with paddy soil. The concentrations of total Hg, MeHg and EtHg in the BC were 29.56 ± 2.12 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , &lt; 0.2 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , &lt; 0.2 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , respectively. The pH values of soil, RS and BC were 5.78 ± 0.01, 7.47 ± 0.18 and 12.42 ± 0.02, respectively. The properties of the additional initial paddy soil, BC and RS were detailed in Table S1 of the Supporting Information (SI). The infrared spectra of paddy soil, RS, and BC are shown in Fig. S1. The chemicals used in this study are detailed in Table S2.

## 2.2. Pot experiment

Rice (the local rice variety, Yongyou 1540, a hybrid indica-japonica with  an  abundance  of  grains,  robust  stems,  and  exceptional  grain quality) was cultivated in the paddy soil to quantify the changes in soil MeHg and EtHg levels. Three treatments were applied in the study: (1) original paddy soil (CK), (2) paddy soil with RS (1.0% w/w), and (3) paddy soil with BC (1.0% w/w). For each treatment, triplicate pots were used, filled with 3 kg of paddy soil and planted with rice (3 plants/pot). All  pots  were placed in a greenhouse (Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, China)  at  ambient  temperature  (15 -40 ◦ C).  The  rice  pot  experiment encompassed  a  complete  rice  growth  season,  spanning  from  transplantation to maturity and harvest, over a duration of 129 days. Detailed information on rice management was shown in Text S1. Soil samples (0 -10 cm) were collected at the maturity stage of rice, and divided into two fractions: one stored at 4 ◦ C for chemical analysis, and the other stored at GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 80 ◦ C for microbial community analysis.

Total  Hg  concentrations  in  soils  were  determined  using  a  Multifunctional Zeeman  effect analyzer  (RA-915 + , Russia) [28]. The geochemical fractions of Hg, including Hg ion (Hg(II)), MeHg and EtHg, were quantified  using  HPLC-ICP-MS  (High-Performance  Liquid  Chromatography coupled with Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry) analysis as described [29]. Further details on the quantification of Hg  speciation  are  provided  in  Text  S2.  Soil  physical  and  chemical properties analysis including pH, total carbon (TC), total nitrogen (TN), total sulfur (TS), organic matter, DOC, and soluble anions (Cl GLYPH&lt;0&gt; , NO3 GLYPH&lt;0&gt; , and SO4 2 GLYPH&lt;0&gt; ) were presented in Text S3. All chemical analyses were performed in triplicate to ensure accuracy. Additional chemical properties are provided in Fig. S2.

For DOM mass spectrometry analysis, DOM in the samples was isolated through solid phase extraction (SPE), and SPE-DOM samples were measured  using  a  Fourier-transform  ion  cyclotron  resonance  mass spectrometry (FT-ICR MS, SolariX 15.0 T, Bruker, Germany), equipped with negative electrospray ionization. The detailed procedures for DOM extraction and FT-ICR MS analyses were detailed in Text S4. The mass spectra  of  FT-ICR  MS  were  depicted  in  Fig.  S3.  DOM  fluorescence spectrum analysis was determined using standard methods, and detailed in Text S5. During the rice-growing seasons, the GHGs were sampled using the static closed chambers method [6,30], and the detailed information can be found in Text S6. In our study, the following indicators can be used to verify soil carbon sequestration: a decrease in labile and easily decomposed organic compounds, an increase in recalcitrant and

Total DNA was extracted from the soil samples using a Fast DNA SPIN Kit for soil (MP Biomedical, USA) following the manufacturer ' s instructions. The extracted DNA was then amplified with bacterial 16S rRNA  gene  (338F-806R)  primer  sets. The original raw sequences collected in this study have been deposited at the NCBI Sequence Read Archive database with accession ID PRJNA1039575 for the bacteria.

## 2.3. Incubation experiment

This  experiment  was  conducted  in  addition  to  pot  experiment  to explore the effects of RS and BC addition on soil carbon sequestration. Three treatments were used for soil incubation experiment: (1) control without amendment, (2) RS amendment (1.0% w/w), (3) BC amendment (1.0% w/w). All 140 ml white culture flasks made of high borosilicate material were sealed and incubated in the dark for 86 days under anoxic conditions in an environmental chamber (28 ◦ C). The incubation scheme  was  designed  to  simulate  the  generally  anoxic  conditions  in flooded paddy soils. There were 45 g of the paddy soil and 58.5 ml of deionized ultrapure water (1:1.3 m:v) in the every culture flask, with a deionized  water  layer  height  of  approximately  1.5  cm.  The  GHGs collection was performed using a syringe on the 86th day of incubation, and the concentration was measured using a gas chromatograph (GC2010 Plus Shimadzu, Japan) within 1 day. Soil samples were collected on the 86th day of incubation, and analyzed for SOC, DOC, CH4, CI GLYPH&lt;0&gt; and NO3 GLYPH&lt;0&gt; (analysis methods are consistent with the pot experiment). The concentrations of MeHg, EtHg and soil key indicators in the incubation experiment were detailed in Text S7 and Fig. S4.

## 2.4. Data quality control and assurance

The standard reference material, including GSS-1a, GSS-2a, GSS-3a, GSS-5a, GBW07915, blanks, and sample replicates were used as quality assurance and quality control (QA/QC) samples. The method detection limits  (3 σ )  were 0.2 ng/g for MeHg and EtHg. The relative standard deviations for duplicate samples were within 6% for MeHg and 4% for EtHg. The recoveries of MeHg and EtHg for were 106% to 125%, and 99% to 119% (n = 5), respectively. RSD for MeHg and EtHg analyses were &lt; 15% (n = 5). Quality control of Hg speciation analysis included sample analysis,  method/reagent  blanks,  matrix  spikes,  and  certified reference materials.

## 2.5. Statistical analysis

The significant differences in chemical characteristics  among different treatments were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) combined with multiple comparisons (Tukey ' s test, P &lt; 0.05). The statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 and visualized with  Origin  2024,  R  (4.3.0)  and  Adobe  Illustrator  2023  software. Redundancy analysis (RDA) was employed to explore the relationship between microbes/environmental factors and DOM  composition.

Furthermore,  network  analyses  were  utilized  to  investigate  the  interactions between microbial populations, revealing the specific links between DOM compounds and bacteria. A false discovery rate (FDR)adjusted p-value threshold of 0.05 was applied in multiple tests and only strong and significant interactions (|r| &gt; 0.7, P &lt; 0.05) were visualized represented. The network visualization and topological properties were computed using the interactive platform Gephi (0.10.1).

## 3. Results and discussion

## 3.1. RS and BC amendments increased soil carbon sequestration

Both RS and BC amendments are prevalent practiced worldwide to enhance soil fertility  and  crop  yields  [32,33].  Our  research  provides compelling  evidence  that  the  application  of  RS  and  BC  significantly enhances  soil  carbon  sequestration,  in  line  with  previous  studies [34 -36]. The average SOC level in RS and BC treatments was 22.78 g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ,  representing  a  5.07%  increase  compared  to  the  CK  (Fig.  1a). Furthermore,  RS  and  BC  amendments  significantly  increased  humus concentration due to the presence of humic-like peaks A and C compared to the CK (Fig. 2a -c). Earlier findings have also shown that RS amendment significantly increased SOC concentration by 8.6% -16.7% during rice growing seasons [6,35]. Furthermore, in our study, the DOC concentration  increased  by  6.03  and  10.27  mg  kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 in  the  RS  and  BC treatments, respectively, compared to the CK. As rice straw decomposes, microbial  carbon  pumps  transform  labile  organic  carbon  into  stable organic forms, facilitating the humification of DOM [37].

In this study, the results indicated that RS amendment increased the quantity of DOM molecules of Lignin by 41.9% and Condensed hydrocarbon by 32.7% compared to the CK (Fig. 2d). This increase is likely due to the high organic matter level of RS, which might facilitate the release of a substantial amount of organic carbon molecules into the soil during  the  decomposition  process.  The  phylum  Chloroflexi  and  the genus Anaerolineaceae were the second most abundant group in the RS (Fig.  3a  and  b). The soil carbon accumulation significantly improved with the BC amendment  ( P &lt; 0.05),  consistent  with  previous  studies  [38,39].  The persistence of soil carbon components, such as Lignin and Condensed hydrocarbon, with BC amendment is crucial when considering BC as a climate change mitigation strategy [11]. The production of BC typically requires additional energy, primarily electricity, which may potentially contribute to an elevation in GHGs emissions. However, life cycle assessments  indicated  that  the  activities  encompassing  the  creation, transportation, and preservation of BC contribute only marginally to the overall GHGs emissions [14]. Incorporating biochar solutions represents a promising strategy for achieving carbon neutrality objectives, while simultaneously  enhancing  food  security  and  environmental  sustainability [11]. It was observed that BC amendment increased the number of DOM molecules of Lignin by 22.9% and Condensed hydrocarbon by 24.7%  compared  to  the  CK  (Fig.  2d  and  f).  This  increase  may  be attributed to the modulation of bacterial community structure and activities  by  BC  application,  influenced  by  the  addition  of  nutrients, recalcitrant  carbon  and  increased  pH  [40].  In  comparison  to  other biomass  types,  BC  led  to  higher  SOC  and  lower  soil  respiration  by increasing the abundance of specific bacteria with lower mineralization or improved humification abilities [41].

The level of Amino sugar in RS and BC treatments were 30.6% and 9.09% lower, respectively, than that in the CK. This reduction was primarily attributed to the significant contribution of microbial necromass carbon to SOC, promoted by microbial growth and activity [1].  Notably,  the  number  of  DOM  molecules  of  Lignin  (3399)  and Condensed hydrocarbon (1014) was much higher than that of Amino sugar (171) (Fig. 2d), indicating that the stable SOC primarily originates from rice root inputs rather than microorganisms, or that microorganisms play a role in carbon decomposition rather than carbon sequestration in this study [42]. Additionally, the study employed FAPROTAX to  predict  bacterial  functions,  revealing  that  the  RS  significantly increased the abundance of Anaerolineaceae by about 20.0% ( P &lt; 0.05), while the BC significantly reduced the degradation of aromatic compounds by 55.9% ( P &lt; 0.01) compared to the CK (Fig. 3c and d).

In summary, our experiment demonstrated that RS and BC amendments significantly enhance the carbon sequestration capacity of paddy soil, supported by comprehensive evidence from SOC to the presence of specific organic molecules (Fig. 6, S4). The organic carbon compounds introduced  by  RS  and  BC  amendments  were  primarily  consisted  of Lignin  and  Condensed  hydrocarbon,  accounting  for  an  average  proportion of 57.2% (Lignin) and 16.9% (Condensed hydrocarbon).

## 3.2. RS and BC amendments affected Hg speciation transformation

The application of RS and BC are common in Hg-contaminated paddy soil  [3,22],  however,  their  effects  on  the  transformation  of  soil  Hg speciation,  particularly  on  EtHg,  remains  poorly  understood.  In  our study, RS amendment significantly decreased Hg(II) concentration by 6.45 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 (88.7%), while increasing MeHg concentration by 0.31 μ g kg GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 (81.6%), indicating a highly efficient conversion from Hg(II) to MeHg (Fig. 1b). Moreover, RS amendment led to an increase in gene copies of DeltahgcA and ArchaeahgcA by 3.80 × 10 8 copies g GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 (42.2%) and  1.18 × 10 8 copies  g GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 (28.4%)  compared  to  the  CK  ( P &lt; 0.05) (Fig. 1c). This could be attributed to the fact that RS contains the highest number of molecules comprising carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen (CHONs) (Fig. 2e), as previous study has demonstrated that CHONs can enhance the abundance (ArchaeahgcA gene) and activity of Archaea [25]. Similar findings have been reported in previous studies, demonstrating  that  RS  amendment  enhances  Hg  methylation  in  paddy  soil [9,10]. This enhancement also could be attributed to the role of RS- derived DOM as an available carbon source for soil microbes, thereby increasing the abundance and activity of microbial methylators [27]. However, the influence of BC amendment on Hg(II) and MeHg concentration  was  not  statistically  significant  in  comparison  to  the  CK, possibly due to the minimal fluctuations in the gene copies of both DeltahgcA and ArchaeahgcA between the BC and CK treatments (Fig. 1c).

Both RS and BC increased the abundance of the hgcA gene; however, the  significant  differences  in  MeHg  accumulation  may  be  due  to  the following reasons: 1) The abundance of the hgcA gene in BC did not significantly differ from that in CK, thus resulting in no notable difference in MeHg concentration between BC and CK; 2) The abundance of the hgcA gene in RS significantly higher than that in CK, leading to a significant increase in MeHg concentration in RS compared to CK; 3) BC significantly  reduced  the  abundance  of  the  DeltahgcA gene  and  the Geobacteraceae compared to RS (Fig. 3c), thereby leading to a notable decrease in MeHg concentration in BC compared to RS (Fig. 1) ( P &lt; 0.05).

Additionally,  the  concentration  of  EtHg  in  our  experimental  soil ranged from 0.05 to 0.20 ng g GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 , consistent with levels reported in the North Rhine floodplain [43], and the Florida Everglades [44]. Both RS and BC amendments significantly decreased the EtHg concentration by 76.4% and 61.1% ( P &lt; 0.05) compared to the CK (Fig. 1b). This is in contrast to the findings of Beckers et al., [43] who implied a limited potential of BC as an immobilization amendment for EtHg, attributing this  limitation  to  the  unclear  understanding  of  EtHg  dissolution  and formation processes.

The observed reduction in EtHg levels in soil following the application of biochar can be attributed to several factors. Firstly, rice plants absorb  EtHg,  thus  contributing  to  its  depletion.  Secondly,  EtHg  may undergo  conversion  into  propylmercury,  phenylmercury,  or  dimethylmercury, which further reduces its concentration in the soil. Additionally, the large specific surface area and abundant functional groups of  biochar  facilitate  the  adsorption  of  EtHg,  thereby  enhancing  its removal from the soil. It was previously believed that the contaminated soil  contained  sufficient  EtHg  binding  sites  (7.05%  of  total  organic carbon) that would minimize the impact of the introduced BC binding sites [43]. However, we contented that the primary EtHg binding sites were  within  the  BC,  not  the  soil  itself  (Fig.  S1).  EtHg  is  not  highly persistent and readily decomposes in the environment, suggesting that the EtHg determined in our experiment may be a result of recent EtHg formation, rather than the release of legacy EtHg [43]. The process of EtHg formation and its behavior in natural environment remains poorly understood, primarily due to the lack of microbial formation data.

Although not commonly reported, EtHg may occur widely in the environment. In our study, the concentrations of EtHg and MeHg were 0.05 -0.20  ng  g GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 and  0.07 -0.38  ng  g GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1 ,  respectively  (Fig.  1b).  The similar levels of EtHg and MeHg indicate that Hg ethylation could be a significant transformation pathway of Hg. EtHg has been detected in soils and sediments, while its presence in other environmental samples such as atmospheric particulates, water, plant, and fish are frequently below detection limits [21,45]. The absence of known sources releasing EtHg into paddy soil implies that its presence in the soil likely results from  natural  microbial  ethylation  processes  occurring  in  flooded anaerobic rice fields. Therefore, the prevalence of EtHg in paddy soil warrants further investigation towards the Hg ethylation process and its role in the biogeochemical cycling of Hg.

Collectively, RS amendment increased SOC, but this was counteracted by a subsequent rise in global warming potential (GWP), thereby negating the benefits of carbon sequestration. Furthermore, RS amendment significantly promoted Hg methylation, consequently heightening the risk of Hg pollution ( P &lt; 0.05) (Fig. 1b, 6a). In contrast, BC amendment significantly increased SOC and concurrently reduced EtHg concentration ( P &lt; 0.05) (Fig. 1a, b, 6b). Overall, BC amendment is more suitable  for  the  remediation  of  Hg-contaminated  paddy  soil  than  RS amendment, considering both carbon sequestration and Hg pollution.

Additionally, the application of BC  inhibits  methanogenic  growth,  a  process  often  stimulated  by  RS application,  consequently  reducing  CH4 release  from  SOC.  Another advantage of using BC is its long-term SOC sequestration potential, as its carbon components decompose at a slower pace than those found in RS [13].

## 3.4. Potential synergistic mechanisms in carbon sequestration and Hg transformation processes

Minimal  research  exists  on  simultaneous  Hg  alkylation  and  soil carbon sequestration. Although the process of soil carbon sequestration may not  directly  participate  in  Hg  alkylation,  under  certain  circumstances, the stabilization of SOC may indirectly influence the progression of Hg alkylation by modulating both the quantity of bioavailable DOM  molecules  and  the  activity  of  Hg-methylating  microorganisms within the soil environment. Previous studies have demonstrated that labile  DOM  participates  in  both  soil  carbon  sequestration  and  Hg alkylation processes [24,50,51]. The MeHg concentration increases in concert with the enhancement of Protein molecular quantity, whereas it diminishes  correspondingly  as  the  quantity  of  Tannin  molecules  increases (Fig. 5a, b), indicating Protein and Tannin may be closely related to the methylation process of Hg. Similarly, Amino sugar, Carbohydrates and Condensed hydrogen may exhibit a close correlation with the ethylation process of Hg (Fig. 5c). These findings support the notion that soil carbon sequestration processes likely exert a significant influence on the Hg methylation and ethylation.

The potential synergistic mechanism between carbon sequestration and Hg transformation processes in this study may be as follows (Fig. 6). Anaerolineaceae facilitate the conversion of labile small-molecule DOM, such as Protein, Amino sugar, and Carbohydrate, into more recalcitrant forms like Tannin and condensed hydrocarbon, thereby enhancing soil carbon sequestration. During the process of soil carbon sequestration, unconverted  Protein,  Amino  sugar,  and  Carbohydrates  that  did  not transform into stable organic matter like Tannin may serve as an energy source for the metabolic activities of Geobacteraceae, thereby promoting the methylation and ethylation of Hg and consequently increasing the concentrations of MeHg and EtHg. The stronger the soil ' s capacity for carbon sequestration, the greater the consumption of labile DOM, which serves as the energy source for Hg-methylating microorganisms; hence, the alkylation of Hg is likely to be weaker.

Previous studies have confirmed that BC plays a role as a soil carbon sequestration and can reduce the Hg methylation [14,39,52]. Our work presents a novel perspective that BC amendment demonstrate a synergistic effect on Hg alkylation and carbon sequestration in paddy soil. Specifically,  BC  application,  mediated  by  Anaerolineaceae,  facilitates the transformation of labile DOM (consisting of Lipid, Amino sugar, and Carbohydrate) into more recalcitrant forms, such as Tannin. Our study based on FAPROTAX analysis validated that BC amendment acts as a carbon storage method through deciphering the functional profiles in bacterial community of the BC treatment, as compared to those of the CK and RS (Fig. 3d). Furthermore, the reduction in Amino sugar and Carbohydrates likely reduced the abundance of Geobacteraceae (Fig. 3c) and thus might inhibit Hg alkylation (Fig. 1b), as Geobacteraceae are

Labile DOM  can  be  utilized by Anaerolineaceae for transformation into stable DOM, while simultaneously providing energy for Geobacteraceae to facilitate mercury methylation. The stronger the soil  carbon  sequestration  capacity,  the  weaker  the  potential  Hg methylation  capacity  may  be.  Our  findings  suggest  that  the  future enhancement of carbon sequestration and mitigation of Hg alkylation are highly likely to be achieved simultaneously by regulating the types and contents of labile DOM, such as Amino sugar and Carbohydrates. Furthermore,  the  inverse  relationship  observed  between  soil  carbon sequestration  and  Hg  alkylation  implies  that  those  regulations  and management practices that are not conducive to soil carbon sequestration could inadvertently increase the risk of MeHg and EtHg in paddy soil.  Thus,  careful  consideration  of  SOC  sequestration  policies  and management is required.

## 4. Conclusions and implications

Many rice fields still suffered from MeHg pollution and insufficient soil carbon sequestration. MeHg is one of the most hazardous pollutants due to its prevalence, persistence, and extreme toxicity. However, the concurrent investigation into Hg alkylation and soil carbon sequestration  remains  a  notably  under-explored  domain.  We  provide  a  novel perspective on the co-mediating influence of DOM and bacteria in carbon sequestration and potential Hg transformation in paddy soil. Results revealed that both BC and RS amendments significantly increased the levels of SOC and recalcitrant compounds, thereby enhancing soil carbon  sequestration  compared  to  the  CK  ( P &lt; 0.05).  BC  amendment appeared to enhance the conversion of a greater proportion of Protein and  Lipid  into  Tannin,  whereas  RS  amendment  showed  a  more

Despite the significant contribution of RS amendment  to  soil  carbon  sequestration,  the  substantial  CH4 emissions resulting  from  the  availability  of  abundant carbon  under  anaerobic conditions negated the potential for carbon accumulation. The Amino sugar, Carbohydrates, and Tannin derived from BC and RS amendments significantly influenced the beta diversity of Geobacteraceae ( P &lt; 0.05), thereby affecting the Hg conversion process. The specific conditions of the study, including climatic environment, soil types, and experimental duration, may influence the outcomes. Further research is thus needed to confirm the generalizability of this findings and explore the mechanisms underlying the observed effects in more detail.  Furthermore,  future  efforts  could  build  upon  our  findings  to explore the impact of soil flooding and drying conditions on this synergistic mechanism.
