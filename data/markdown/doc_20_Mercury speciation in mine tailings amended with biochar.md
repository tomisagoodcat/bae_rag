## a b s t r a c t

Biochar is a low-cost and environmentally friendly amendment with strong ability for adsorption of mercury (Hg) from aqueous solutions, contaminated soils, and sediments. In the present study, six biochars were prepared from the pyrolysis of cocoa pod husk, sugarcane bagasse and banana pseudostem at 400 and 600 °C in order to use them as an organic amendment and to evaluate their capacities to reduce the bioavailability, methylation potential, and mobility of Hg present in mine tailings without environmental treatment. To quantify the effects of each variety of biochar, incubation experiments of soil were established by mixing mine tailings with 5% by weight of biochar for 90 days. Once the incubation time concluded, sequential extraction procedures were carried out to determine the fractionation of the Hg species. Speciation analysis results indicated that the remedial effects of biochar depended on the source of organic matter and pyrolysis temperature. The bioavailable and organic Hg fractions decreased respectively by up to 75 and 79%, indicating a methylation potential reduction. Immobile Hg fraction increased to 76% with respect to the control. Adsorption and stabilization to HgS from the soluble forms of Hg reduce the percentage of bioavailable Hg. The organic Hg fraction reduction was correlated with the decrease of the bioavailable Hg fraction and with direct adsorption processes in the biochar structure. Highly porous biochars developed at high temperature, with large contents of super /uniFB01 cial polar functional groups (H/C), and high pH, electrical conductivity, ash percentage and cation exchange capacity values favor the stabilization and adsorption of Hg in mine tailings. In summary, the application of biochar could be an effective method for the remediation of Hg-contaminated mine tailings, transforming the Hg species into less toxic, soluble, reactive, and bioavailable forms.

Although all forms of Hg are toxic, organomercurial species such as methylmercury (MeHg) are subtle neurotoxins that bioaccumulation and biomagni /uniFB01 cation in food webs (Liu et al., 2012).

Artisanal and small-scale gold mining (ASGM) have been identi /uniFB01 ed as the main anthropogenic source of mercury to the environment at a global level (Esdaile and Chalker, 2018; UNEP, 2013; Velásquez-López et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2012). It is estimated that between 410 and 1400 metric tons of mercury are emitted each year through ASGM, representing 37% of the global mercury emissions (Esdaile and Chalker, 2018). Hg releases associated with mining activity affect approximately 10 to 19 million miners around the world (Esdaile and Chalker, 2018). In the ASGM, elemental Hg (Hg 0 ) is used to extract and separate gold from ore (Esdaile and Chalker, 2018). Due to the low modernization and poor environmental control present in ASGM during the mineral bene /uniFB01 ciation processes, only the 50% of the Hg 0 is recovered and reused, approximately 4% is evaporated to the atmosphere, and from 26 to 82% is retained in the mine tailings (Cordy et al., 2011). As a /uniFB01 nal stage, mine tailings are disposed in land /uniFB01 lls with little or no environmental management (Ebinghaus et al., 1999; Opiso et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2012).

Chemical speciation of contaminated sites is a critical process for understanding and modeling metal contamination systems (Bloom et al., 2003). ## 2. Mine tailings as source of Hg contamination

Once the ore is extracted, a /uniFB01 rst stage of Hg dispersion may occur in the processing center by accidental spills of metallic Hg 0 or by active vaporization of gaseous Hg 0 during amalgam burning (Lacerda and Salomons, 1998; Telmer and Veiga, 2009). Although mine tailings are supposed to be kept completely isolated, its inappropriate environmental management will allow the release of Hg into the environment (Fig. 2). Because metallic Hg 0 is volatile under aerobic conditions, passive degassing of Hg can occur on the mine tailings surface once it is deposited on the ground (Ebinghaus et al., 1999; Windmöller et al., 2015). Theremaining metallic Hg 0 in the deposit will oxidize to Hg 2+ over time (Boszke et al., 2008; Durão Júnior et al., 2009; Skyllberg, 2011). Oxidized mercuric Hg 2+ readily forms inorganic mercuric salts and minerals (e.g., HgCl2, HgO, or HgS) or, under appropriate conditions, organomercurial compounds (e.g., methylmercury, phenylmercury) (O'Connor et al., 2019), increasing Hg bioavailability, toxicity and mobility. Although Hg leaching from tailings is a slow process, over the years it may become in the most important dispersal mechanism (Ebinghaus et al., 1999). Until then, erosion processes of mercury-contaminated particles are the mechanisms that govern the dispersion of Hg from the mine tailings (Durão Júnior et al., 2009; Ebinghaus et al., 1999).

Both in-situ and ex-situ innovative technologies have been used for Hg remediation from contaminated soils and mine tailings (Wang et al., 2012). However, they can be technically challenging, highly expensive and not practical for large contaminated sites (Kabata-Pendias, 2011;

## 3. Materials and methods

## 3.1. Mine tailings sampling area

The mine tailings were collected at a depth of 0 -15 cm from an artisanal gold mine (8°25 ′ 7.87 ″ North and 74°13 ′ 26.75 ″ West) located in the Bolivar mining district (Colombia), which has been exploited since pre-Hispanic times. Currently, gold mining in this region is mostly controlled by artisanal and small-scale mining. Previous studies developed in the department of Bolivar by UPME et al. (2015) and Rocha-Román et al. (2018) exposed the close relationship between mercury-based ASGM and agricultural soils, crops, water bodies, and biota contaminated with Hg and methylmercury.

## 3.2. Characterization of mine tailings

The pH and electrical conductivity (EC) values of the mine tailings were measured in distilled water at a ratio of 1:2 (by weight) soil/ water with a Hach HQ30d multimeter (21.4 °C). Textual characterization and moisture content were established following the standard methods D422 -63 (ASTM, 2002) and D2216-10 (ASTM, 2010), respectively. The mineral phases in the mine tailings were characterized by Xray diffraction analysis, using a BRUKER D8 ADVANCE diffractometer, operating in Da Vinci geometry and equipped with an X-ray tube (CuK α 1 radiation: λ = 1.5406 Å, 40 kV and 40 mA), a 1-dimensional LynxEye detector (with an aperture angle of 2.93 o ), a divergent slit of 0.6 mm, two soller axials (primary and secondary) of 2.5° and a nickel /uniFB01 lter. Data collection was carried out in the 2 θ range of 2 -70°, with a step size of 0.02035° (2 θ ) and counting time of 0.6 s/step. Phase identi/uniFB01 cation was performed using the software Match! Version 3.10.2.173 and the free-of-charge crystallography open database (COD) from the

University of Cambridge. Total Hg (HgT) determination was based on APHA (2017).

## 3.3. Production and characterization of biochars

Six biochars were prepared from the pyrolysis of Colombian agroindustrial wastes (cocoa pod husk Theobroma cacao , sugarcane bagasse Saccharum of /uniFB01 cinarum and banana pseudostem Musa paradisiaca ) at low (400 °C) and high (600 °C) temperature (Dong et al., 2013). The biochars were prepared by slow pyrolysis (10 °C/min) in muf /uniFB02 e furnaces, without the use of nitrogen, in oxygen-free conditions, and maintaining the maximum temperature for 2 h. Previously to pyrolysis, the organic matter was chopped and air-dried for seven days. The detailed method for preparing biochar is described in Ok et al. (2015) and Tan et al. (2017).

Prior to characterization, biochars were crushed and sieved to less than 2 mm. pH and electrical conductivity (EC) values were measured in a biochar/water slurry at a 1:10 ratio using a Hach HQ40D Portable Multi Meter (21.4 °C). The cation exchange capacity (CEC) was measured by the ammonium acetate method using a solution of 1 N CH3COONH4 at pH = 7 (Zgorelec et al., 2019). The ash content was measured following the standard methods ASTM-D2866 (ASTM D2866, 2018). The elemental composition of carbon (C), hydrogen (H), nitrogen (N), and sulfur (S) was determined using an elemental analyzer Vario ElCube. Secondary electron (SE) imaging and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) analysis were carried out using an FEI QUANTA FEG 650 environmental scanning electron microscope (ESEM), under the following analytical conditions: magni /uniFB01 cation = 100 -4000×, WD = 9.4 -10.7 mm, HV = 25.00 kV, signal = SE, detector = ETD, EDS Detector EDAX APOLO X with the resolution of 126.1 eV (in. Mn K α ).

## 3.4. Incubation of Hg-contaminated mine tailings with biochars

Thesoil incubation experiment was established mixing 5% by weight of biochar in 100 g of mine tailings in polypropylene pots for 90 days (He et al., 2019). The percentage of biochar to be mixed was based on studies conducted by O'Connor et al. (2018) and Bussan et al. (2016). The incubation experiments were carried out at room temperature

(24 to 26 °C) allowing oxygen in the system. During the incubation period, the moisture of the mine tailings was kept constant at 11.4% by adding deionized water every 7 days based on weight loss (Jin et al., 2016). A control experiment was prepared without the amendment (mine tailings +0% biochar). The control experiment underwent the same treatments as the mine tailings mixed with biochar. Soil incubation experiments began 130 days after the mine tailings were generated in the mining site. During this time, the mine tailings were kept outdoors, just as it would be in the real mine deposit. In total, 7 different matrices were prepared in triplicate for incubation: mine tailings +0% biochar (control experiment); mine tailings +400SB; mine tailings +400CPH; mine tailings +400BP; mine tailings +600SB; mine tailings +600CPH and mine tailings +600BP. Before mixing, both matrices (biochar and mine tailings) were air-dried for seven days at room temperature, sieved through a 2 mm sieve, and homogenized.

## 3.5. Hg speciation in incubated soils

Speciation of Hg was determined following the sequential extraction procedure proposed by Bloom et al. (2003). This sequential extraction protocol differentiates the Hg species into behavioral classes, de /uniFB01 ning consecutively the water-soluble Hg, organic acid-soluble Hg, organic Hg, elemental Hg and Hg sul /uniFB01 des (residual fraction). All reagents used were analytical grade. All the necessary preparations were performed with deionized water. The bioavailable Hg fraction was determined from the sum of the water- and organic acid-soluble Hg fractions (Bloom et al., 2003). The methylation potential was correlated with the organic Hg fraction (Bloom et al., 2003). Since Hg 0 and HgS are highly immobile and low reactivity species (Matsumoto and Liu, 2020; Liu et al., 2019), these two fractions were combined into a single fraction denominated immobile Hg (Matsumoto and Liu, 2020). The biochar particles were not separated from the mixture for the sequential extraction procedure. All experiments were performed in triplicate. In Table 1. ## 3.6. Mercury measurement

Mercury was measured by hydride-generation atomic absorption spectroscopy (HGAAS) following the methodology 3112B de /uniFB01 ned in APHA (2017). Before measurement, all samples were treated by an acid digestion process using HNO3 and N2S2O3, subsequently were oxidized with KMnO4 and K2S2O8, and heating slowly and uniformly at 95 °C for 2 h in a closed system. The samples were preserved using HNO3 and K2Cr2O7. The measures in HG-AAS were obtained using NaBH4 and HCl at 30%. All the analysis were made in less than 15 days.

## 3.7. Statistical analysis

The correlation analysis was evaluated using the Pearson ( r ) correlation coef /uniFB01 cients between bioavailable Hg, organic Hg, and immobile Hg fractions. The correlation importance was de /uniFB01 ned with a con /uniFB01 dence level of 95% ( p &lt;0.05). For the correlation analysis, all speciation results of the mine tailings + biochar matrices were used, regardless of the biochar variety ( n =18). All calculations in this study were carried out using the IBM SPSS 25v software.

## 4. Results and discussion

## 4.1. Mine tailings

Mine tailings exhibited a sandy silty texture, low permeability and 11.4% moisture. Their pH and EC values are 2.92 and 1610 μ s/cm, respectively, which indicates moderately acidic to very acidic conditions. According to X-ray powder diffraction analysis (results not shown), the mine tailings are mainly composed of quartz (SiO2) and sul /uniFB01 des such as pyrite (FeS), chalcopyrite (CuFeS2) and sphalerite (ZnS). HgT concentration ranged from 700 to 1027 mg/kg, overcoming the HgT concentration worldwide average in soils (1.1 mg/kg), con /uniFB01 rming the high level of contamination in the mine tailings (UPME et al., 2015).

Physical and chemical characteristics of biocharsIn general, biochars have neutral to alkaline pH (6.11 -10.01). Biochars produced from Musa paradisiaca registered the highest pH (&gt;9.90). The CEC is an important parameter which reveals extent of sites on the biochar surface that can potentially retain cations by electrostatic forces (Zgorelec et al., 2019). 400BP and 600BP showed the highest % ash and CEC. ## 5. Conclusions

The main issue with Hg-contaminated mine tailings with no environmental treatment is the long term potential for release of Hg into the environment. This study demonstrated the potential of biochar to decrease Hg bioavailability, toxicity, and mobility through the development of less toxic, reactive, soluble, and therefore much less availabe Hg species. One of the most important facts found in this research was the ability of biochar to reduce the potentially mobile Hg species (bioavailable Hg and organomercurial), which consequently reduces the risk to develop Hg long-lasting leaching into the environment. Additionally, as bioavailable Hg fraction recorded a strong positive correlation with organic Hg fraction, biochar could also serve as a control method of Hg methylation through the decrease of Hg bioavailability. The proposed mechanisms for Hg immobilization in mine tailings amended with biochar are mainly associated with formation of Hg sul /uniFB01 des from bioavailable Hg (HgCl2, HgSO4 and HgO), co-precipitation of Hg chlorides or oxides, and physical adsorption of HgS and organic Hg compounds on or within the biochar structure. The ef /uniFB01 ciency to stabilize and adsorb Hg is associated with the type of biochar used, being favored by high- temperature and porous biochars with high molar ratios O/C vs H/C, pH, EC, % ash, and CEC. The morphology of biochar also plays an important role in the Hg adsorption. Highly porous biochar structures, with large surface area, and high super /uniFB01 cial polar functional groups content generated active surface areas to immobilize Hg. Organic matter source and pyrolysis temperature were the most important variables of biochar, since these de /uniFB01 ne the characteristics of biochar associated with Hg immobilization processes.
