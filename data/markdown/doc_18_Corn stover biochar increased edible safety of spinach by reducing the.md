## a b s t r a c t

Mercury (Hg) is toxic and can affect human health through soil entering food chain. Spinach absorb easily heavy metals. Corn stover biochar can improve soil structure and physicochemical property. This study wanted to establish a Hg-corn stover biochar-soil-spinach model including 1 control group (without HgCl2 and corn stover biochar) and 24 treatment groups (with HgCl2 or/and corn stover biochar). Hg concentration was 0, 1, 2, 4, and 6 mg kg -1 , respectively. Corn stover biochar contents were 0%, 1%, 3%, 5%, and 7% w /w, respectively. The results showed that residual Hg concentrations was the largest and water soluble and exchangeable Hg as well as carbonate bound Hg concentrations were the lowest among /uniFB01 ve Hg forms. Hg concentrations in four Hg treatment groups were higher than the control group in dose-dependent manner. The deposition of 6 mg kg -1 Hg was the highest. Corn stover biochar decreased Hg migration from soil to leaching solution and spinach, and passivation effect of 7% concentration of corn stover biochar was the best. Besides, corn stover biochar relieved the increase of methyl Hg caused by Hg in soil. Moreover, Hg concentration in roots was the highest and Hg concentration in stems was the lowest in spinach. Furthermore, Hg absorbed by roots was more than the sum of Hg absorbed by stems and leaves. In addition, we also found that the measured soil Hg concentrations were coincided with the predicted soil Hg concentrations under 1, 2, and 4 mg kg -1 Hg concentrations, except 2 mg kg -1 Hg at 7% C. Under 6 mg kg -1 Hg concentration, measured soil Hg concentrations was lower than that of the predicted soil Hg concentrations. Taken together, our /uniFB01 ndings indicated that corn stover biochar can increase edible safety of spinach by immobilizing Hg in soil and be used as an organic amendment.

## 1. Introduction

Mercury (Hg), an environmental pollutant, is listed as one of the ' top ten chemicals of concern ' andis harmful to human beings (WHO, 2017). Under natural conditions, Hg comes from geologic sources (Obrist et al., 2018). Human activities, such as chlor-alkali process, cement production, mining, smelting, artisanal small-scale gold mining, coal burning, and oil re /uniFB01 ning cause environment pollution. To reduce Hg emission from industrial activities, the Mercury and Air Toxics Standards is enforced by governments worldwide (Yang et al., 2019b). The global amount of Hg accumulation in soil is very large, assumed to be in the range of 250 -1000 Giga grams (Obrist et al., 2018). Two Giga grams of anthropogenic Hg are released into the environment per year (UNEP, 2009). Hg concentration ranged from 6.5 to 790 mg kg -1 in surface mining-area soil in Guizhou Province, China (Li et al., 2009). The accumulation of Hg in agricultural soil not only can cause Hg contamination of soil, but also can affect food quality and food safety. The soil polluted by Hg can lead to Hg deposition in agricultural crops (Qian et al., 2009). Accordingly, reducing the migration of Hg from soil to crop is urgent and a social responsibility for sustainable agriculture and public health.

Leafy vegetables are likely to absorb and accumulate heavy metals. Spinach ( Spinacia oleracea L.), one of the widely edible vegetables, is a kind of plants with green leaves and tender stem. It is also a nutritious vegetable rich in carotenoid, vitamin C, vitamin K, and iron. Zubair et al. (2019) found that Hg stress resulted in Hg accumulation and damage in spinach.

In order to reduce the harm of plants caused by Hg pollution in soil, it is an effective way to reduce the migration of Hg from soil to plants. Corn stover biochar, as a soil amendment, can diminish Hg mobility in soil and prevent food chain transfer. Corn stover biochar is thermally produced with corn stover under oxygen-limited conditions. The advantage of corn stover utilization is its wide abundance and low cost, and therefore provide a plentiful, renewable, and cost-effective feedstock for biochar production. According to statistics, maize yield in the world was 1038.28 million tons which was much higher than those of rice and wheat in 2014. Meanwhile, based on the residue to crop ratio of 1.6, the yield of corn stover in the world can reach 1661.25 million tons (Liu et al., 2018). In China, approximately 216 million tons of corn stover is produced every year (Yang et al., 2019a). Nevertheless, less than 10% of corn stover is processed for industrial production, animal feed, and bedding (Kim and Dale, 2004). Most of corn stover is left unused, discarded optionally or burnt directly, thus wasting corn stover resources and polluting environment. Thus, we wanted to use corn stover biochar as soil amendment to reduce Hg pollution. Corn stover biochar with high stability and aromatizing has high organic carbon and aperture structure, a large surface area with various functional groups, and higher cation-exchange capacity. Fuertes et al. (2010) reported that biochar from corn stover pyrolysis at a peak temperature of 550 °C was highly aromatic and had low H:C andO:Cmolarratios.Also, corn stover biochar contains high ash content with high potassium concentration mainly. Besides, corn stover biochar was used as an adsorbent to eliminate the need for KOH activation for the manufacturing of activated carbon. Corn stover biochar also provides supplemental nutrients including calcium, magnesium, and iron (Shen et al., 2015).

Principal chemical forms of Hg in soil are ionic Hg (Hg 2+ ) and organic Hg. Hg entering the soil forms various speciation with different stability by various chemical reactions with soil components. The various Hg species in soil have distinct properties, affecting the fate and transport of Hg species in soil. On one hand, biochar can impact Hg mobility and transfer in plant-soil systems, which decreases the amount of Hgavailable for crops by changing the chemistry and binding properties of Hg in soil (O'Connor et al., 2018). For example, Turull et al. (2019) evaluated the effectiveness of biochar on the mobility of different Hg compounds by selecting an agricultural soil. Xing et al. (2019) explored the mechanism of the migration of Hg from soil to rice and Hg accumulation in rice after biochar was added to soil. On the other hand, biochar can effectively immobilize Hg through affecting Hg form, ion exchange, surface complexation, and coprecipitation, and prevent Hg migration in soil (Dong et al., 2013). Dong et al. (2013) found that cation exchange was involved in Hg sorption by Brazilian pepper biochar, and pointed out that carboxyl and phenolic hydroxyl groups were the main binding sites of pyrolytic biochar under 300 °C and 450 °C, while carboxyl group and graphite structure were the main binding sites of pyrolytic biochar under 600 °C. Zhang et al. (2019) investigated Hg adsorption capacities and negative shift of biochar obtained from various materials. Therefore, corn stover biochar can be used as a chemical passivator for Hg-contaminated soil to convert Hg to a stable form to reduce the mobility of Hg according to the physicochemical properties of biochar.

The migration of toxic heavy metals (such as Hg) from soil to plant is affected not only by the types of heavy metals, biochar, and soil, but also by the species of plants. Biochar can mitigate toxic effects of nickel (Younis et al., 2015) and copper (Coumar et al., 2015) on spinach. However, whether corn stover biochar can reduce the harm of Hg on the soil and plants is unknown. Hence, we intend to study mitigative role of corn stover biochar added in soil on the harm of Hg on spinach through investigating the form of Hg, the concentration of methyl Hg, the migration of Hg from soil to spinach, and the distribution of Hg in spinach. The speci /uniFB01 c aims of this study were: (1) to demonstrate the effect of corn stover biochar addition to soil on methyl Hg (MeHg) and Hg fractions; (2) to investigate the deposition of Hg in soil and the relationships of Hgbetweensoil, leaching solution, and spinach; (3) to explore corn stover biochar addition to soil on Hg accumulation, distribution in spinach, and the relationships of Hg between adjacent spinach tissue structures (i.e., root, stem, and leaf); (4) to reveal biochar addition to soil on the effect of Hg migration from soil to spinach. This paper will provide a basis for reducing soil Hg risk and increasing food safety.

## 2.3. Analytical methods

Cation exchange capacity (CEC) and soil pH were determined using sodium acetate /uniFB02 ame photometry method (Bao, 2000) and using a pH meter (Sartorius PB-10), respectively. Soil samples (2 g per sample) were digested with a mixture of sulfuric acid and perchloric acid at mixed rate of 3:1, then digested samples were diluted with 100 mL of distilled water. Further, 5 mL of solution from this digest was used to determine total nitrogen (TN) using the macro-Kjeldahl method and total phosphorus (TP) using spectrophotometry as described by Zhao et al. (2018). Speci /uniFB01 c surface area of biochar was determined by JW-BK112X, Beijing, China, according to the instructions provided by the equipment manufacturer.

Tessier /uniFB01 ve-step continuous extraction method was used to analyze Hg chemical forms in soil. For water soluble and exchangeable Hg, 16 mL of MgCl2 with a concentration 1 M (pH = 7) was added into the conical /uniFB02 ask with a 2.00 g soil sample and was shaken well. After continuous oscillation at room temperature for 1 h, the sample was centrifuged at a speed of 4000 r min -1 for 10 min. At last, extracted supernatant, 10 mL of K2Cr2O7 preservation solution, and the prepared K2Cr2O7 diluent were transferred to 50 mL brown volumetric /uniFB02 ask to measure water soluble and exchangeable Hg.

The soil samples were digested with a solution of concentrated acid. Digested samples (0.5 g over 0.15 mm sieve per sample) were added into a digester bottle containing a mixture of HNO3 (analytically pure (A.R.)), HCl (A.R.), and H2O mixed with a concentration of 1:3:4 ( v / v /v) ratio. The solution was heated in water bath at 95 °C for 2 h to detect Hg concentrations in the soil. The samples of spinach roots, stems, and leaves (0.5 g per dried sample) were put into a mixture of HNO3 (A.R.) and H2SO4 (A.R.) (4:1, v/v) and digested in water bath at 95 °C for 2 h to detect Hg concentrations of roots, stems, and leaves. Hg concentration in soil and plant was tested using thermo Fisher ice-3500 (Shenzhen, China) according to the instructions provided by the equipment manufacturer.

MeHgconcentration in soil was determined using high performance liquid chromatography-inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (HPLC-ICP-MS). Soil samples (5.0 g per sample) were weighed and were placed in centrifuge tubes, then 20 mL of nitric acid solution (A.R.) with a concentration of 0.5 mol L -1 were put into the tubes. Then the tubes were put into the Prosonic 1000 automatic ultrasonic liquid treatment device for 1 h and was centrifuged at a speed of 4000 r min -1 for 5 min. 5.0 mL of supernatant was put into a 25 mL beaker, and then 1:9 ( v /v) ammonia solution was added to adjust the pH value to 7.5. ## 4. Discussion

The bioavailability of Hg in soil affects the absorption of Hg by plants, especially chemical forms of Hg (Residual Hg, organic matter bound Hg, Fe and Mn oxide bound Hg, carbonate bound Hg, as well as water soluble and exchangeable Hg) in soil can re /uniFB02 ect bioavailability of Hg. Fe and Mnoxide bound Hg with relatively low availability is dif /uniFB01 cult to be separated (Lacal et al., 2003). Fe and Mn oxide bound Hg and organic matter bound Hg are called potential available Hg, and can be transformed into plant available forms under certain conditions. In our experiment, the addition of corn stove biochar alleviated the increase of Fe and Mn oxide bound Hg, and organic matter bound Hg in soil caused by Hg treatment. In the wake of the increase of corn stover biochar addition, the concentration of Fe and Mn oxide bound Hg and organic matter bound Hg reduced, thereby reducing the potential bioavailability of Hg. Carbonate bound Hg is unstable, is easily in /uniFB02 uenced by environment, and is decomposed into Hg, HgO, and Hg2O. Carbonate bound Hg is positively correlated with soil pH, the more soil pH values, the more carbonate bound Hg concentrations. Our data also supported the above mechanism. In this study, corn stover biochar addition increased soil pH value. Hg treatment, and both Hg treatment and corn stove biochar addition increased carbonate bound Hg concentrations in soil. Moreover, the concentration of carbonate bound Hg in the combined group was higher than that in the Hg group, meaning that Hg was not easily transported from soil to plants. Residual Hg with the lowest bioavailability and no mobility is stable and is not absorbed by plants. Hg in soil is mostly bound to solids, and only a small proportion of 0.2% (Xu et al., 2014) or 0.3% (Chen et al., 2016) is assigned to aqueous solution. Cui et al. (2012) found that Hg was absorbed by plants mainly in the form of water soluble and exchangeable Hg. Water soluble and exchangeable Hgwith the strongest migration and transformation is available Hg, which can be easy to be absorbed and utilized by soil biology. Therefore, we also investigated residual Hg and water soluble and exchangeable Hg. We found that more corn stover biochar enhanced the more increase of residual Hg caused by Hg, and alleviated the increase of water soluble and exchangeable Hg caused by Hg. Moreover, residual Hgwasthemain,accountedfor 61.8%under C1Hg4,while water soluble and exchangeable Hg was the least, accounted for 2.8%. Our above /uniFB01 ndings suggested that the addition of corn stover biochar to soil reduced the migration of Hg from soil to plants and 7% of corn stover biochar reduced more effectively the migration of Hg.

MeHgisthemostextensiveorganic mercury in theenvironment and has high water solubility. Organic matter can reduce the bioavailability of Hg, especially high-level organic matter may lead to inorganic Hg into a stable complex and loss of bioavailability, which is not conducive to methylation (Gorski et al., 2008). Under the condition of a large amount of biochar composting, soil MeHg decreased (Darby et al., 2016). Corn stove biochar improved effectively the soil organic matter and available nutrients contents (Zhao et al., 2020). In this study, corn stover biochar contained 227.20 g kg -1 organic carbon and reduced the increase of MeHgcaused by Hg treatment in soil, meaning that inhibiting Hg methylation in soil. In addition, Steffan et al. (1988) elucidated that Hg dissolved easily under acidic conditions resulted in more bioavailable Hg and the methylation of inorganic Hg, which in turn under alkaline conditions Hg methylation and MeHg concentrations reduced in soil. It was consistent with ours that corn stover biochar increased pH value and decreased the increase of MeHg concentration induced by Hg, demonstrating that corn stover biochar decreased MeHg migration from soil to plant and 7% of corn stover biochar has the lowest MeHg concentration.

Biochar is a high pH organic material and has a capability to potentially reduce the metal bioavailability in soils. Corn stover biochar has a large speci /uniFB01 c surface area that may bind to cations, usually alkaline, thus reducing Hg 2+ solubility and the concentration of water soluble and exchangeable Hg that can be absorbed directly by spinach, increasing the concentration of residual Hg, improving the adsorption performance in soil. Therefore, Hg was being adsorbed in soil, thus decreasing Hg migration from soil to plant. The above mechanism supports our results. In this study, corn stover biochar addition reduced the increase of Hg concentration induced by Hg treatment in the root, leaf, and stem of spinach. Moreover, the more corn stover biochar, the less Hgconcentration in the root, leaf and stem, and 7% was the best. Diverse parts of same plant such as root, stem, and leaf have different functions and structures, maybe have different absorption ability of Hg. The bioampli /uniFB01 cation of heavy metal in plants mainly depends on the absorption of root, and the accumulation of leaf and stem. MarrugoNegrete et al. (2016)found that 24 plants accumulate the most Hg in the root, followed by the leaf and stem. We also found the similar results. Under Hg treatment and corn stover biochar addition, the order of Hg concentration in spinach tissues was roots &gt; leaves &gt; stems, and Hg concentration of roots were higher than the sum of leaves and stems, manifesting that the accumulation of Hg was mainly in root. The reason maybe as follows. Spinach root absorbs Hg in soil. There are a large number of exchange sites in the cell wall of spinach root, Hg ions can be immobilized at these sites, which prevent Hg ions from further transferring to the upper of spinach. The complexation of plants and heavy metals make Hg enriched in roots, thus dif /uniFB01 cult to transport to the aboveground of plants (Krupp et al., 2009). In addition, deionized water was used for watering during our spinach growth experiment to avoid the in /uniFB02 uence of Hg in water on the experimental results. Abad-

Valle et al. (2016) also used deionized water to avoid the effect of water onheavymetals.Deionized water was also measured and there were no Hg in deionized water in our experiment.

In our experiment, Hg concentration in soil increased after corn stover biochar addition and 7% of corn stover biochar was the best. The possible mechanisms are as follows:

- i. In our experiment, corn stover biochar changed Hg speciation. Water soluble and exchangeable Hg as well as MeHg abated, meaning that the loss of Hg from soil reduced. Besides, residual Hg in soil increased, meaning that Hg in soil increased. In addition, corn stover biochar increased pH and CEC, reducing water soluble and exchangeable Hg, furthermore demonstrating that the loss of Hg from soil reduced.
- ii. Wefoundthattheproperties of corn stove biochar affected Hg deposition and transformation. Generally, the surface area of adsorbent can affect the adsorption capacity of Hg (Yang et al., 2019c). In our experiment, corn stove biochar with high speci /uniFB01 c surface area and abundant pore structure can absorb Hg ions. Meanwhile, high pH of corn stover biochar was found, meaning that high pH promoted alkali ion exchange reaction, precipitation and complexation of biochar and Hg ions. Additionally, biochar which is produced at high temperature has a relatively stable and insoluble carbon pool, which reduces metal mobility (Awad et al., 2018). Organic carbon content is one of the most important factors that determining the mobility of potential toxins in soil (Chen et al., 2019). High organic carbon contents were found in corn stover biochar in this study, meaning that Hg adsorbed on corn stover surface increased and Hg was not easily absorbed by spinach.
- iii. Corn stover biochar can change the soil characters and increase Hg concentration in soil in this study. Biochar application can increase pHand form heavy metal precipitation (Houben et al., 2013). Moreover, pH value can affect the forms of heavy metals by impacting the solubility of heavy metal compounds in soil (Martínez and Motto, 2000). Additionally, high pH value can impact the adsorption position, stability of adsorption, existing forms, and coordination properties of heavy metals, result in the changes of chemical forms of heavy metals in soil (Rees et al., 2014). In addition, Windmöller et al. (2015) found that large amounts of organic matter in soil helped the oxidation of Hg 0 to Hg 2+ and hindered the reduction of Hg 2+ to Hg 0 . Besides, the chemisorption/oxidation of Hg 0 played a more important role than the physisorption (Yang et al., 2016). Our addition of corn stover biochar increased organic matters in soil, and the increased organic matter may affect the redox of Hg in the soil.
- iv. Corn stover biochar addition decreased leaching Hg concentration and Hg leaching in our experiment. In addition, corn stover biochar reduced Hg concentration in root of spinach and also supported that corn stover biochar increased concentration of soil Hg.

Additionally, in our experiment, we found that the measured soil Hg concentrations were identical to the predicted soil Hg concentrations under 1, 2, and 4 mg kg -1 Hg concentrations, except C4Hg2 treatment, indicating that the effect of corn straw biochar on soil Hg concentration can be predicted in the range of 1 -4 mg kg -1 Hg using Eq. They studied the measured and estimated concentrations of heavy metals in farmland soil after dairy manure fertilization and found that based on the estimated outcomes of the mass balance model for each heavy metal, the estimated tendencies of Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, Mn, and Zn were basically accordant with the results of /uniFB01 eld monitoring. Meanwhile, in our experiment, we also found that, under 6 mg kg -1 Hg concentration, measured soil Hg concentrations was lower than predicted soil Hg concentrations and decreased with the increase of corn straw biochar. However, Qian (2020) found the results of /uniFB01 eld monitoring of As and Hg was higher than the estimated contents. Above situations need to be further study.

Furthermore, we researched the characteristics of corn stover biochar, including the speci /uniFB01 c surface areas, pH, and organic matters contents. The speci /uniFB01 c surface areas, pH, and organic matters contents at 400 °C were 186.7 m 2 g -1 , 9.57, and 22.72%, respectively. Others also investigated that the properties of corn stover biochar. The speci /uniFB01 c surface areas, pH and C element contents of corn stover biochar at 300 °C were 1.4 m 2 g -1 , 8.16, and 79.97%, respectively (Mireles et al., 2019); at 400 °C and 600 °C were 5.42 m 2 g -1 and 320.77 m 2 g -1 , 8.80 and 11.27, 76.02% and 90.78%, respectively (Zhang et al., 2016); and at 550 °C were 13.08 m 2 g -1 , 10.54, and 74.46%, respectively (Wang and Liu, 2017). Besides, the speci /uniFB01 c surface areas of biochar determined by Zhang et al. (2016) and Chen et al. (2011) at 600 °C were 320.77 m 2 g -1 and 13.08 m 2 g -1 , respectively. It can be seen from the above data that above three properties varied greatly. There were few studies on the inactivation of heavy metals in soil by corn stover biochar. Zhang et al. We researched the immobilization of Hg in soil at 400 °C by using corn stover biochar. Corn stover biochar-induced the inactivation of heavy metals needs to be further studied.

In addition, we found that with the increase of biochar, soil Hg and residue Hg increased, Hg in leaching solution and spinach, MeHg, and water soluble and exchangeable Hg decreased, and 7% biochar had the best effect. O'Connor et al. (2018) also found that treatment dosages of 1%, 2%, and 5% of S-modi /uniFB01 ed biochar were found to reduce freely available Hg in soil. This was consistent with our research.

## 5. Conclusions

Hg treatment increased Hg concentration in soil, leaching solution, and spinach. Hg deposition in soil under 6 mg kg -1 Hg treatment was the highest. Meanwhile, corn stover biochar added to soil increased Hginsoil and reduced Hg uptake of spinach, while raised the concentration of residual Hg and carbonate bound Hg, however decreased water soluble and exchangeable Hg and Hg leaching loss. The effect of 7% corn stover biochar addition on the reduce of Hg concentration was the best in spinach. In addition, the highest Hg concentration of spinach tissues was found in root, followed by leaf and stem. The accumulation of Hg in root was more than the sum of leaf and stem of spinach. As well as, Eq. (1) can be used as predicting the effect of corn straw biochar on soil Hg concentration under the range of 1 -4mgkg -1 . In conclusion, the addition of corn stover biochar reduced MeHg concentration in soil and Hgmigration from soil to spinach, which indicated that corn stover biochar was an effective method to reduce health risk caused by Hg in spinach.
